Mini Biography: Lovish Bhatia

Lovish Bhatia is a computer science researcher, educator, and technology enthusiast with a strong interest in Quantum Computing, Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning.

Academic Journey

He completed his Master of Computer Science from Chandigarh University and is currently pursuing an M.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering at the National Institute of Technology Delhi.

His academic journey is driven by curiosity and a desire to understand how mathematics, computation, and emerging technologies can be combined to solve complex problems.

Research & Achievements

His research interests include Quantum Machine Learning, Digital Forensics, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, Space Weather AI, and Algorithmic Optimization.

He has worked on research problems involving hybrid quantum-classical neural networks, digital-forensics applications of AI, image classification, and scientific applications of machine learning.

One of his important research interests is Quantum Computing, where he explores how quantum algorithms and quantum machine learning can contribute to future computational systems.

Technical Expertise

He works with technologies and frameworks such as Python, Qiskit, PyTorch, TensorFlow, Keras, and machine-learning frameworks.

His work combines concepts from computer science, mathematics, artificial intelligence, and scientific computing.

Correction Corner

Computer science is only about programming. It is also about algorithms, mathematics, problem-solving, research, and understanding how computational systems can be used to explore complex problems.

Interesting Examples

His interests connect concepts such as quantum states, neural networks, and mathematical models. For example, quantum computing uses concepts such as qubits, while machine learning relies heavily on mathematical operations such as matrices and vectors.

Ideas such as 2n also become particularly interesting in quantum computing because an n-qubit system can represent a superposition involving up to 2n computational basis states.
